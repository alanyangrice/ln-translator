## 8. Pacing
The prose compresses school days, breaks, and repeated visits quickly: “A few days later,” “Golden Week came and went,” or “the sports festival came and went.” In contrast, single physical moments are expanded into granular sequences of touch, reaction, and interpretation, sometimes stretching one bite, lick, or near-kiss over many paragraphs. Mundane routines—elevator rides, serving drinks, handing over money, preparing instant food—are given steady attention because they mark changes in the relationship.

Conflict scenes move slowly, with frequent pauses for internal explanation before the next action. Dialogue exchanges can be brisk, but the narration often stops afterward to evaluate tone, posture, or what the words imply. This creates a rhythm where external events are small, but emotional and bodily consequences take up most of the space.
