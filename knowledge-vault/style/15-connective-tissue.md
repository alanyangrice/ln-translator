## 15. Connective tissue
The prose links thoughts with explicit logical connectors: “However,” “That being said,” “Because of that,” “After all,” “In any case,” “As a result,” and “Instead.” Characters frequently explain their own reasoning step by step, even when the reasoning is self-serving or flawed. This makes the narration feel methodical, almost argumentative.

These connectors should express the English logic of the paragraph, not mechanically preserve the order or connective shape of the Japanese. If the natural English version needs to reorder a setup, action, and conclusion, do so while keeping the same causal and emotional relationship. Associative leaps do happen, but they are usually signposted with conversational pivots like “Oh, right,” “Come to think of it,” or “Speaking of which.” Bare juxtaposition is rare; the translator prefers to state the bridge between ideas. Even emotional contradictions are connected through phrases like “Despite that” or “And yet.”
