## 13. Sensory emphasis
Touch dominates the sensory field. The prose repeatedly describes wetness, warmth, stickiness, teeth against bone, nails digging into skin, fabric against the body, and temperature differences between rooms and bodies. Taste is also important in specific scenes: chocolate sweetness, salty fingers, soda fizz, cup noodles, and blood like “rusty iron.”

Visual detail is functional rather than decorative: clothing, buttons, hair, uniforms, rooms, and textbook covers are noticed because they signal social role or intimacy. Smell appears less often but carries emotional weight, especially Sendai’s shampoo lingering on Miyagi’s pillow. Sound is used for domestic quiet—page turning, intercoms, chopsticks, pencils—rather than lush atmosphere.
