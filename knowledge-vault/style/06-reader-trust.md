## 6. Reader trust
The prose explains motivations and causal links more than it implies them, often spelling out why someone acts: “That was why,” “Because of that,” “The reason for that was.” It frequently restates relationship conditions—money, orders, rules, school secrecy—so the reader is not left to infer the arrangement’s mechanics. Emotional causality is also often narrated directly, though the narrators themselves may deny the obvious conclusion.

The style trusts the reader less with plot logistics than with emotional contradiction. It will clearly explain where someone sits, what bill changes hands, and why an order technically follows the rules, but it leaves labels like friendship, jealousy, desire, and affection contested. Much of the ambiguity comes from self-deception rather than omitted information.
