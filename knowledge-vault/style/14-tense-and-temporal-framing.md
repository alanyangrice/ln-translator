## 14. Tense and temporal framing
The base tense is first-person past tense, with habitual past used heavily for routines: “Whenever,” “Usually,” “There were times,” and “Up until now.” Flashbacks are introduced plainly, often with “Back when,” “The first time,” or “I thought back to,” and then folded into the current emotional situation. The narration frequently contrasts present scene behavior with what used to be normal.

Hypotheticals and future projections are common: whether they will meet until graduation, attend different universities, or stop seeing each other after a break. Time skips are handled directly—“A week had gone by,” “Once July was over”—without elaborate transitions. Text messages anchor time in concrete repeated rituals.
