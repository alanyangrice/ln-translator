## 5. Narrative distance
The narration stays close to the POV character’s immediate perceptions, especially during touch, pain, embarrassment, or silence. It rarely feels like an older narrator looking back with mature hindsight; instead, the narrator interprets events from inside the scene, using limited guesses like “I had no idea,” “most likely,” or “perhaps.” The prose frequently editorializes, but those judgments are personal and unstable rather than authoritative.

Distance narrows during physical contact, where actions are broken down into small sensations: tongue, bone, breath, skin temperature, pressure. It widens during explanations of rules, school hierarchy, family situations, or how the arrangement began. These shifts are usually explicit, moving from “I felt…” into “That was why…” or “The reason for that was…”
