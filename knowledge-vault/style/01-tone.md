## 1. Tone
**Sendai:** Sendai’s narration is dry, socially alert, and exasperated, with irritation often softened by teasing or practical commentary. She can be blunt about annoyance—“Seriously, what a hassle”—but rarely lets emotion stay raw for long before converting it into analysis, jokes, or logistical concerns. When hurt or unsettled, the tone becomes heavier and more self-conscious, especially around money, family, and Miyagi’s avoidance.

**Miyagi:** Miyagi’s tone is curt, defensive, and possessive, with loneliness and anxiety deliberately buried under “it doesn’t matter” logic. She often frames cruelty as entertainment or contract enforcement, but the narration leaks unease through bodily phrasing like “my heart felt tight” and “I felt restless.” Emotional intensity is controlled by denial: she repeatedly insists things are “not a big deal,” “not jealousy,” or “that was all there was to it.”
