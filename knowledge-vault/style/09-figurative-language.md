## 9. Figurative language
Figurative language appears regularly but is not decorative or dense. The translator uses conventional, functional comparisons to clarify a feeling: “needle in a haystack,” “sleep spell,” “like a dog,” “like a pigeon,” or “rusted in place.” More original images appear when the narrator is emotionally unsettled, such as girls as “colorful cakes” with “poison,” feelings vanishing “like soda bubbles,” or Sendai as a wary “stray cat.”

The style usually avoids elaborate metaphor chains. Similes tend to be practical, comic, or slightly awkward in a way that preserves a Japanese light-novel flavor, as with the retained “cat’s forehead” / “mouse’s forehead” comparison. Physical sensation is described more literally than metaphorically.
