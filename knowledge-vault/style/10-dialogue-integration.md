## 10. Dialogue integration
Dialogue is frequent and presented in Japanese-style brackets 「」, with text messages in 『』. The prose usually surrounds dialogue with small actions, facial reactions, or tonal tags: someone “said curtly,” “sounded unamused,” “mumbled,” or “replied coldly.” Dialogue rarely stands alone for long; the narrator almost always comments on what was said or what it reveals.

Attribution is simple and repetitive, relying heavily on “said,” “asked,” and “replied,” often followed by participial action: “said as she…” or “asked while…” Bare exchanges occur during arguments, but even then, a paragraph of internal interpretation usually follows. The effect is dialogue embedded tightly inside the narrator’s emotional processing.
