## 3. Sentence structure
The translator favors medium-length explanatory sentences joined by “but,” “so,” “because,” and “however,” often stacking cause, qualification, and self-correction into one line. Short sentences appear for emphasis after longer reasoning: “No response,” “She obeyed silently,” “It was delicious,” or “This is strange.” Paragraph rhythm often alternates between dialogue, a brief physical action, and a longer internal explanation of what that action means.

Fragments and repetition are used sparingly but noticeably for comic or emotional stress: “Once. Twice. And then a third time,” or “Boyfriend, boyfriend, boyfriend.” A common pattern is statement-then-revision: “I wasn’t angry. Or rather…” / “It wasn’t that… But…” This gives the prose a slightly translated but consistent introspective cadence.
