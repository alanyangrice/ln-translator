## 3. Sentence structure
The translator favors natural English sentences that preserve the narrator's sequence of thought: cause, qualification, denial, self-correction, and conclusion. These relationships are often linked with “but,” “so,” “because,” and “however,” but the English does not need to preserve Japanese clause order. When a source sentence stacks too many turns for natural English, split it into readable sentences while keeping the same emotional progression.

Short sentences appear for emphasis after longer reasoning: “No response,” “She obeyed silently,” “It was delicious,” or “This is strange.” Paragraph rhythm often alternates between dialogue, a brief physical action, and a longer internal explanation of what that action means. Fragments and repetition are used sparingly but noticeably for comic or emotional stress: “Once. Twice. And then a third time,” or “Boyfriend, boyfriend, boyfriend.” A common pattern is statement-then-revision: “I wasn’t angry. Or rather…” / “It wasn’t that… But…” The cadence should feel deliberately introspective, not like English forced into Japanese syntax.
