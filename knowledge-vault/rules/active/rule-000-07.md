# Rule

Imitate the sentence rhythm and prose patterns of the human reference parts in the sliding window — not just produce "valid English." Match the translator's close, plain, emotionally segmented rhythm: dialogue and short physical beats are often followed by a separate sentence or paragraph of guarded interpretation. Do not mistake this for permission to preserve every Japanese clause inside one long English sentence.

## Patterns to imitate

- **Shaped narration over terse summary.** Preserve the narrator's reasoning, but shape it into English sentences that can be read aloud naturally. The goal is the reference translator's cadence, not a clause-by-clause mirror of the JP.
- **Split stacked monologue when English needs it.** If a JP sentence stacks setup, objection, correction, and reluctant concession, split it into two or three English sentences when a single sentence would ramble or flatten the voice. Keep every source beat, implication, and emotional turn; splitting is not permission to summarize, omit, or add emphasis.
- **Use short sentences for Miyagi's resistance.** Miyagi's narration often lands on blunt, defensive conclusions after explanation. Let lines like "That was all." / "I didn't like that." / "There was no reason to..." stand plainly instead of burying the point in a long connective chain.
- **Contractions throughout narration and dialogue.** "isn't / it's / I'd / wouldn't" rather than "is not / it is / I would / would not", unless the reference window is using the longer form for emphasis.
- **Cushioned dialogue tags, not vague voice labels.** Pair attributions with the speaker and action ("Sendai-san asked brightly as...") when the speaker is clear. Avoid generic metonymy like "I heard a cheerful voice" unless the source is deliberately withholding who spoke.
- **Beat-and-then structure.** Where the reference uses a paragraph break or a "Then, ..." beat, keep that beat instead of mashing two thoughts into one compound sentence.

## Priority order

- Preserve the source's meaning, agency, and emotional turn.
- Reorder clauses freely when English needs a different setup-action-conclusion order.
- Use the reference translator's rhythm after the sentence is already natural English.
- Do not let a retrieved precedent, style-profile phrase, or source clause order override readability.
