# Rule

Use 「…」 and 『…』 **only** for what the human reference uses them for, and use English curly quotes ("…") for everything else.

- **Use 「…」** for primary spoken dialogue, exactly as the human reference does.
- **Use 『…』** for nested quotations, embedded quotations, and text messages.
- **Use English curly quotes ("…")** for non-dialogue scare quotes, labels, emphasis quotes, and mid-narration paraphrases (e.g. the narrator referring to a concept by name).

Never substitute English curly quotes for actual spoken dialogue, and never use 「」/『』 for non-dialogue emphasis.

## Why

Two complementary failure modes:

1. **Reaching for English convention inside dialogue.** When a remembered or paraphrased *line* surfaces mid-narration, the model often switches to "…" even though the surrounding chapter uses 「…」 for speech.
2. **Over-applying Japanese brackets to non-speech.** The model also over-corrects — wrapping scare quotes, concept labels, or emphatic phrases ("the *little bet*") in 「」 even though those aren't dialogue. The human reference uses English curly quotes for those.

Keep brackets consistent with what's actually being quoted, not with the surrounding language convention.

## Examples

- **Dialogue (use 「」):** `「今日呼んでないんだけど」` → `「I didn't ask you over today.」`
- **Nested / messages (use 『』):** A text message rendered inside narration → `『家に来てる』` → `『I'm at your place.』`
- **Scare quote / concept label (use "…"):** `“小さな賭け”` → `"little bet"`, **not** `「little bet」`.
