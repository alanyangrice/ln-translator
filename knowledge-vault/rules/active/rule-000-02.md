# Rule

Prioritize natural English that preserves the source's function, emotional force, and level of commitment. Faithful translation is not word-for-word equivalence: when the Japanese relies on an idiom, social judgment, metaphor, discourse marker, or omitted subject, choose the English phrasing a novelist would actually use. The boundary is invention: do not add facts, motives, named emotions, sexual implications, quantities, or stronger/weaker register that the Japanese does not support.

## Decision Boundary

- **Good interpretation:** the English changes the surface form but preserves what the JP is doing in the scene: social pressure, tact, jealousy, understatement, embarrassment, deadlock, etc.
- **Bad addition:** the English adds new content or changes degree: a stronger emotion, a named motive, a relationship label, a sexual implication, a quantity, or a register shift.
- If natural English needs an implied subject/object that the JP leaves implicit, supply it from context. That is required English grammar and is governed by `rule-011-08`.

## Examples

- 再生速度を速める → "increased the playback speed", not "double the speed".
- 変態 → "pervert" alone — do not add "who got off on..." or other sexual-gratification implications the source does not state.
- 距離感がバグっている → "has no sense of boundaries" / "has no sense of personal space", not "something is wrong with her sense of distance". This is equivalent social meaning, not added meaning.
- 平行線を辿る話し合い → "a discussion going nowhere" / "talking past each other", not "a parallel-line discussion". This preserves the metaphor's function without inventing new content.
