# Rule

Render Japanese idioms and figurative expressions with equivalent English idioms or natural English phrasing rather than translating literally. When a literal rendering would be unnatural, misleading, or read as something other than what the source means, find an idiomatic equivalent.

The failure mode this rule prevents: rendering the *surface state* of the Japanese idiom (continuing to borrow, being poisoned, throat being moistened) instead of the *social or psychological meaning* a native English speaker would actually express.

## Examples

- 毒されてる → "something is messed up about me" / "I've been corrupted", not "I'm being poisoned" (unless the context is literal poison).
- 機嫌が悪くなる → "put in a bad mood" / "sour the mood", not "pissed off" unless the source register is genuinely that strong.
- 借りっぱなしが嫌 → "I don't like feeling indebted" / "I don't want to owe you", not "because I don't want to keep borrowing it" (the JP idiom is about the social obligation, not the literal borrowing state).
