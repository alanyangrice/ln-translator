# Rule

When the Japanese uses a vague descriptive noun for an object that is clear from the immediate physical context, render the concrete contextual referent in natural English instead of calquing it as “things,” “lumps,” “kernels,” or another awkward literal label.

## Applies When

The JP uses a vague noun such as もの, これ, それ, やつ, 塊, or another descriptive label while the scene has already established the concrete object.

## Do

- Name the concrete object an English reader would naturally use in that scene.
- Use a pronoun if the object is already obvious and repeating the noun would sound stiff.
- Preserve any important tone in the vague label: dismissive, playful, embarrassed, or evasive.

## Avoid

- Do not calque vague nouns as "thing(s)" when English would name the object.
- Do not over-specify beyond the scene. Choose the known referent, not an invented detail.
- Do not preserve a JP descriptive label if it makes the sentence sound like an object taxonomy.

## Examples

- カモノハシのティッシュカバー → "the platypus tissue box cover" when the cover matters; "the platypus" / "the tissue box" when the scene has made the object obvious.
- 背中からティッシュが生えたこれは → "the tissue box with the crocodile cover", not "this thing with tissues growing from its back" unless the joke itself is being foregrounded.
- 部屋の主 → "Miyagi" / "the girl who actually lived there", not "the owner of the room."
