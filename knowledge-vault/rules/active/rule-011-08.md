# Rule

For Japanese zero-subject clauses, infer the omitted subject from the POV, context, and established action rather than automatically carrying over the nearest named character.

## Applies When

Japanese omits the actor, experiencer, possessor, or target, especially in internal monologue, action beats, and descriptive narration. The nearest named person is often not the subject.

## Do

- Resolve the missing subject/object from the POV character, scene blocking, and the previous established action.
- If English needs an explicit pronoun or possessor for naturalness, add the contextual one. This is not an added fact under `rule-000-02`; it is required English grammar.
- Preserve whose knowledge, feeling, or action the JP assigns, even when English needs a fuller clause.
- When JP uses a relative position like `隣`, `向かい`, or `目の前` and the person is already established, name the person or use the natural pronoun. Do not replace a known character with generic wording like "the person beside me."

## Avoid

- Do not default to the nearest named character.
- Do not flip agency to make the sentence smoother.
- Do not hide an omitted subject behind a vague passive if the scene makes the actor clear.

## Examples

- 名前も知らない相手から → "from someone whose name I didn't even know", not "without introducing themselves first" if the point is that the POV character lacks the other person's name.
- 感情を露わにした私 → "I let my emotions show" / "my emotions were exposed", not "she dragged my emotions out."
- 街灯の下、隣に向かって文句を言う → "I complained to Sendai-san as she walked beside me under the streetlight", not "I complained to the person beside me" and not the stiffer "I complained to Sendai-san beside me" when Sendai-san is already the clear addressee.
