# Rule

Translate 健全じゃない in judgments about relationships, orders, or behavior as “absurd,” “not right,” “messed up,” or another idiomatic moral/social judgment rather than literal “unhealthy.”

## Applies When

健全じゃない is used to judge a relationship dynamic, an order, a demand, or someone's behavior as socially/morally off. It is not describing physical health.

## Do

- Choose the English moral/social judgment that fits the speaker's register: "absurd," "not right," "messed up," "wrong," or "not normal."
- Keep the intensity matched to the line. Miyagi's restrained internal narration may prefer "not right"; sharper dialogue may allow "messed up."

## Avoid

- Do not write "unhealthy" unless the context is literally about physical or psychological health.

## Examples

- 健全じゃない関係 → "an absurd relationship" / "this relationship isn't right", not "an unhealthy relationship" when the issue is social/moral discomfort.
- 健全じゃない命令 → "a messed-up order" / "an order that wasn't right", not "an unhealthy order."
